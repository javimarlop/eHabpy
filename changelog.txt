- New segm_optim/setup.py: creates the GRASS GIS project, named after the study area file in pa/, from the GeoTIFFs in ../inVars (with their CRS), imports them and the study area in pa/ into PERMANENT, writes palist.csv and fills the REQUIRED options of config.py (including RESOLUTION and the NoData values) from the data.

- The results of moranvar_plots.R are saved in results/<STUDY_AREA> (e.g. results/guajares, with results/guajares/p25, p50 and p75 when SIMIL = 3), so the results of each study area are kept apart. FORCE_RESTART only removes the results of the study area being processed, and csv/shp/tiffs are started from scratch when they belong to another study area (recorded in csv/study_area.csv).
- segmentation_pca_par.py merges the segments smaller than minarea with the most frequent segment within 3 km in memory (numpy/scipy) instead of creating three raster maps per segment and joining them with r.patch, which failed with "Too many open files" when masked inputs leave thousands of isolated fragments and took about an hour per threshold. The result is the same cell by cell (checked on the Guajares data, 4076 small segments).
- The radius used to merge segments smaller than minarea is now SMALL_SEGM_RADIUS in config.py, in pixels (default 5, i.e. 50 m with 10 m pixels), instead of a fixed 3 km, so it depends on the pixel size. Each small segment joins the most frequent of the larger segments within that radius, in repeated passes until nothing changes, so groups of nearby fragments end up in one segment; the small segments left (no larger segment within the radius) then join the nearest segment with minarea cells or more, at any distance, so no segment smaller than minarea is left.
- ehab_segm_core.py no longer skips the segments that start in the first row or column of the input rasters (offset 0 was taken as outside): when the study area plus PA_BUFFER reaches the north or west edge of the variables, those segments got no statistics and were left out of the HFTs (19 % of the area in the Guajares data).
- HFT maps (moranvar_plots.R): the study area outline is drawn in black (it was yellow, like one of the HFT colours) and the segment borders in thin grey, so the outline stands out.
- HFT maps (moranvar_plots.R) are drawn with ggplot2 (geom_sf), 12 inches wide at 300 dpi, with the legend outside the map, coordinates and a title with the study area, threshold, cut and number of HFTs.
- Radar plots (moranvar_plots.R): the variable labels leave out the parts of the name shared by all variables (e.g. the study area prefix and the mask suffix) and are split into several lines, with more room around the radar and a fixed size (10 x 7.5 inches at 300 dpi), so long variable names are no longer cut.
- Radar plots have the same title as the HFT maps: study area, ID, threshold, cut (p25, p50 or p75 with SIMIL = 3) and number of HFTs.
- New boxplots per HFT (moranvar_plots.R, boxplot_<id>_<threshold>[_<cut>]_hft_pixels.png): one panel per variable with its own scale and original values, one box per HFT from the values of all its pixels (read from ../inVars; whiskers to 1.5 x IQR), the colours of the HFT map and the area of each HFT (ha and %) in the legend.
- New heatmaps of the HFT profiles (moranvar_plots.R, heatmap_<id>_<threshold>[_<cut>]_hft_profiles.png): median of the pixels of each HFT for every variable, coloured as z = (median - mean) / sd of the study area, with the variables ordered by eta2 (share of their variance explained by the HFTs) and the HFTs in the order of the dendrogram.
- New PCA biplots (pca_biplot_<id>_<threshold>[_<cut>]_hft.png): PCA of the standardized pixel values (PC1-PC2 and PC1-PC3) with the HFT centroids sized by area, ellipses with half of the pixels of each HFT (up to 10 HFTs) and the variables as vectors labelled on an outer ring. environment.yml adds r-ggrepel for these labels.
- NMDS plots (moranvar_plots.R, NMDS_<id>_<threshold>[_<cut>]_segms_mean.png) are drawn with ggplot2: each segment coloured by HFT with the map colours and sized by area, joined to the HFT centroid, 68 % ellipses with up to 10 HFTs, the variables fitted as vectors with vegan::envfit (r2, grey when p >= 0.05), the stress in the subtitle and the HFT areas in the legend. metaMDS runs with a fixed seed, so the NMDS is the same on every run.
- With SIMIL = 3 the HFT colours follow the hierarchy of the classification tree (hier_colours() in moranvar_plots.R): the HFTs of the cut with fewest classes keep their Set1 colour, the HFTs of the finer cuts inside each of them are shades of that colour from dark to light in the order of the dendrogram, and an intermediate HFT takes the middle shade of the HFTs it contains, so a group keeps its colour across p75, p50 and p25. The legends (map, radar, boxplots, NMDS) and the boxes of the boxplots are in the order of the dendrogram, so the shades of a colour are together (new group.order option in CreateRadialPlot.R).
- New run_hft.py and hft_state.py: every step records in segm_optim/run_state.json the inputs it was run with (options of config.py, palist.csv, input files and its own code). run_hft.py runs only the steps that are out of date and the steps after them (--status shows what would run and why, --from repeats from a step), and each script run by hand stops with a message saying what to run first when a step before it is out of date.
- The steps can be run again safely: get_mean_median_var.py and moranvar.py replace their csv files instead of appending rows to them (running them twice duplicated the rows).
- segmentation_pca_par.py resumes an interrupted run only when its inputs did not change, starts from scratch otherwise, and does nothing when it is up to date. FORCE_RESTART is now False in config.py and only forces a new segmentation.
- PA_BUFFER is in map units, as documented: g.region grow counts pixels, so PA_BUFFER = 500 grew the region by 500 pixels (5 km with 10 m pixels). It is now divided by RESOLUTION and rounded up.
- The working region and the regions of the segments are aligned with the grid of the first variable (g.region align=), so the masks of the segments fall exactly on the pixels of the variables (before, the grid followed the edges of the polygons, with a pixel size slightly different from RESOLUTION).
- ehab_segm_core.py: the statistics of the segments are computed in double precision and no longer rounded to 2 decimals, the noise added to the pixel values is seeded with the segm_id (same results on every run) and the pixel offsets are rounded instead of truncated.
- setup.py stops when the variables have a geographic CRS, imports again the files that changed since it imported them and records what it imported.
- moranvar_plots.R moves the previous results of the study area to results/<STUDY_AREA>_previous before writing the new ones, so files of runs with different options are not mixed; moranvar.py stops with an error when moranvar_plots.R fails.
- README: extended tutorial of the HFT module, interpretation of all the outputs and annex on how the model works.
- Scenarios: new SCENARIO option in config.py (default 'base'); the results are written to results/<STUDY_AREA>/<SCENARIO> (and the previous ones of the scenario to <SCENARIO>_previous). run_hft.py shows the scenario and, in a terminal, lets you change it before starting (--scenario NAME sets it without asking), and warns, listing the options that differ, when the scenario already has results made with other options.
- New SEGM_PCA option in config.py (default True, the previous behaviour): with False segmentation_pca_par.py segments the variables of ENV_VARS0 themselves instead of the first three axes of their PCA, copied to the working region of each ID so that i.segment scales them to their range in that region, as it does with the components. It is an input of the segmentation in run_state.json, so changing it repeats the segmentation.
- ehab_segm_core.py no longer adds a random noise of up to 1/RESOLUTION to the pixel values before computing the statistics of the segments. It was meant to avoid failures with variables that are constant in the whole study area, but it raised the means by 0.05 on average (0.1 at most) with 10 m pixels and was about 18 % of the variance inside the segments for NDWI in the Guajares data (threshold 0.4). It will be added again only where such a failure shows up (e.g. a Mahalanobis distance).
- New THRESHOLD_METHOD, THRESHOLD_ALPHA and MORAN_NEIGHBOURS options in config.py. moranvar.py now chooses the segmentation threshold (before, moranvar_plots.R with its option optim) with one of: 'f', the F-function of Johnson et al. (2015) as in the GRASS addon i.segment.uspo (default, with THRESHOLD_ALPHA = 0.5); 'gs', the Global Score of Espindola et al. (2006); 'gs_fixed', a Global Score with fixed bounds (variance inside the segments over the total variance plus |Moran's I|), which does not depend on the thresholds tested; 'crossing' and 'crossing_first', the original first crossing of the curves minus one (previous default, optim = 2) and the first crossing (optim = 1); or a fixed threshold. It writes the measures and score of every threshold and the one chosen to csv/<id>_threshold.csv, copied to <id>_optim_thresholds.csv with the results (before, the crossings). In a test with the Guajares data 'f' with alpha 0.5 chose 0.5 with every range of thresholds tested and leaving out any of the variables, while the original method chose 0.7 and moved with the highest threshold tested. MORAN_NEIGHBOURS = 'queen' also counts as neighbours the segments that only touch at a corner, which leaves fewer segments without neighbours when many pixels have no data. moranvar.py computes Moran's I without permutations (only I is used) and builds the neighbours once per threshold.
- New output of moranvar_plots.R: r2_<id>_hfts.png, r2_<id>_hfts.csv and r2_<id>_cuts.csv, the variance of the pixel values explained by the HFTs (R², the eta2 of the heatmap, mean over the variables) when the Ward tree of every threshold is cut into 2 to 30 or more HFTs, with the cuts of the selected threshold marked. It is computed from the pixels of ../inVars, so it is exact with STAT_AGG = 'mean' and 'median'. The threshold plot (<id>_moran_var_mean.png) shows the score of the method and the threshold selected instead of the unused average curve.
- New CLASS_METHOD option in config.py for the classification of the segments into HFTs (moranvar_plots.R): 'ward_area' (default), Ward's method weighted by the number of pixels of each segment on its mean (or median) of every variable standardised with the standard deviation of the pixels, the same tree as Ward's method on all the pixels starting from the segments; 'ward_area_kmeans', the same followed by k-means weighted by area; and 'ward', the original unweighted Ward on the means and variances rescaled 0-1. In a test with the Guajares data (thresholds 0.3 to 0.7, 2 to 20 HFTs) 'ward_area' gave a higher R² and silhouette than 'ward' in all the cases and a higher mean Jeffries-Matusita distance between HFTs in 84 % of them. Its tree has the highest joins at the top, so the cuts of SIMIL = 3 give fewer HFTs (9, 3 and 2 instead of 19, 8 and 3 at threshold 0.5). The NMDS and the cophenetic correlation use the distances between segments of the method, and the R² curves classify every threshold with it.
- With SIMIL = 3 the classification tree is also cut at 10 and 90 % of its height (folders p10 and p90, besides p25, p50 and p75), to have a more detailed and a broader set of HFTs without running the model again. In the Guajares example (threshold 0.5, CLASS_METHOD = 'ward_area') the five cuts give 29, 9, 3, 2 and 2 HFTs.
- New hierarchical colours of the HFTs with SIMIL = 3, as the "tree colors" of Tennekes & de Jonge (2014) in HCL: every HFT of the broadest cut gets a part of the colour circle and the HFTs of the finer cuts inside it share it out, with alternating luminance between neighbouring HFTs. Before, the HFTs of the finer cuts were shades of the colour of the broadest cut, which with 2 HFTs in p90 left the 29 HFTs of p10 in shades of two colours. Luminance and chroma were chosen with a palette validator: palettes of up to about 10 HFTs keep the neighbouring colours of the legend distinguishable, also with colour blindness.
- moranvar_plots.R suggests one of the cuts: the one whose number of HFTs is closest to the bend of the R² curve of the selected threshold (Kneedle, 2 to 30 HFTs), beyond which more HFTs add little explained variance. It is printed, written at the end of processing_metadata.txt, marked in r2_<id>_hfts.png and in the new columns r2_share (R² of the cut over the R² of the segments) and suggested of r2_<id>_cuts.csv. In the Guajares example it suggests p25 (9 HFTs, 75 % of the R² of the segments).
- The files of the suggested cut carry _optim after the name of the cut (e.g. p25/map_<id>_<k>_p25_optim_segms_hclust.png and park_segm_<id>_<k>_p25_optim_class.shp), and the subtitle of its figures says "suggested". The suggestion is now computed before the cuts are written.
- New output of moranvar_plots.R with SIMIL = 3: hft_tree_<id>_<k>.png, the tree of the HFTs of the five cuts, from p90 (top) to p10 (bottom), with every HFT as a circle (its number and colour of the maps, sized by area) joined to the HFTs of the next cut it shares segments with (line width: shared area). It shows which HFTs of a cut belong to the same HFT in the broader cuts and how each one splits in the more detailed ones; with CLASS_METHOD = 'ward_area_kmeans', whose cuts are not exactly nested, the opacity of each line is the part of the lower HFT that comes from the upper one. hft_tree_<id>_<k>.csv gives, for every HFT of p10, the HFT that contains it in every cut, with its number of segments and area.
- moranvar_plots.R no longer draws the NMDS of the segments (NMDS_<id>_<k>[_<cut>]_segms_mean.png). Its envfit r2 did not measure the importance of the variables for the HFTs and was easily misread: it is the part of each variable (segment means, unweighted) explained by a linear gradient across the two NMDS axes, the same in every cut, and the three correlated vegetation indices set those axes. In the Guajares example (threshold 0.5, p25) the vegetation indices had r2 = 0.88 to 0.93 but eta2 = 0.28 to 0.38, as only 40 to 50 % of their pixel variance is between segments, and slope had r2 = 0.05 although it separates HFTs 3 and 7. The eta2 of the heatmap (also in r2_<id>_cuts.csv) gives the importance of each variable, and the PCA biplot shows how the HFTs are distributed.
- New INVARS_FOLDER option in config.py (default '', the GeoTIFFs directly in ../inVars): the name of a subfolder of ../inVars with another set of input variables, to try it without removing or moving the files of ../inVars. setup.py imports the variables of that folder, with the folder name in front of the GRASS map names (sin_twi/ndvi.tif -> sin_twi_ndvi) so that the maps of several folders can be in the project together, and fills ENV_VARS0, ENV_VARS (files as sin_twi/ndvi.tif) and RESOLUTION with them. run_hft.py runs setup.py when INVARS_FOLDER changes (also when setup.py left no record), before comparing the scenario, and then every step. SCENARIO is now '' by default: an empty SCENARIO is the name of INVARS_FOLDER (other characters than letters, digits, - and _ become _), or 'base', so every set of variables gets its own results folder. run_state.json keeps the files imported from every folder, so going back to a folder only imports the files that changed. The status of run_hft.py and hft_state.py also shows the folder of the variables.
- New output of moranvar_plots.R: cor_<id>_vars.png and cor_<id>_vars.csv, the Pearson correlation between every pair of variables in the pixels of the segments of the selected threshold (lower triangle in the order of ENV_VARS, |r| >= 0.7 in bold), so that the user knows which variables repeat the same gradient, which counts more than once in the classification (in the Guajares example NDVImax, NDVImin and NDWI, r = 0.73 to 0.88). The README explains it, with an example figure.
- New CLIP_PERCENTILE option in config.py (default 0, no clipping): every layer of i.segment (PC1-PC3, or the variables with SEGM_PCA = False) is clipped to its percentiles CLIP_PERCENTILE and 100 - CLIP_PERCENTILE in the working region (r.quantile and r.mapcalc in segmentation_pca_par.py), and moranvar_plots.R classifies the segments with their statistic and variance computed from the pixels with every variable clipped to its percentiles in the pixels of the segments. i.segment scales each layer to its range and the classification standardises each variable with its standard deviation, so without clipping a few extreme pixels set how much each layer weighs. The statistics of the segments, the threshold selection, the R² and all the plots use the original values. The option is recorded for the segmentation and the HFTs only when it is not 0, so it does not make the steps already run out of date; status messages show 'not set' for options at their default. In a test with the Guajares data (0.5) the segments were a little more homogeneous for the same number of segments (mostly in slope), but the selected threshold moved from 0.5 to 0.6 and the HFT map changed a lot (ARI 0.38 for p25) without better HFTs overall; in the classification alone it hardly changed the HFTs.
- New segm_optim/get_vars_gee.py and its recipe gee_vars.yaml: downloads the input variables from Google Earth Engine with the catalog of the MonitorEO app (OBSNEV), for the study area of pa/ (grown by PA_BUFFER) with a fine pixel size, in ../inVars/<folder>, and for the union of the ecoregions of RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017 that the study area touches with a coarser one, in ../inVars/<folder>/ecoregion (with ecoregions.gpkg; setup.py ignores the subfolder). The recipe gives the variables (of the catalog, of any other collection, or fixed images such as a DEM with terrain: slope), period, season, pixel filters, landcover classes to leave out (ESA WorldCover) and one or several metrics per variable, over the whole period or by year, month or 16 days and then between the years. The files are numbered in the order of the recipe, aligned to grids with edges at multiples of the pixel size, with NoData -9999; gee_vars.json records what each file was made with, so only the files whose recipe or grid changed are downloaded again. --list shows the catalog, --dry-run the grids, sizes, ecoregions and number of images, --use sets INVARS_FOLDER. Each grid is downloaded directly, in tiles computed on demand, or with drive: true (or --drive) by export tasks to a folder of Google Drive (monitoreo/drive.py), without limits of size or time per tile, for big grids such as the ecoregions at 100 m: the script waits for the tasks, imports the files from Google Drive for desktop (or --drive-dir) and joins the pieces onto the grid; running it again resumes the tasks already started instead of starting them again.
- New monitoreo folder: the Python core of MonitorEO (Earth Engine Python API), usable by other tools. catalog.json is the catalog of the app, read from its published source by tools/import_catalog.py. It ports the collections of each sensor (Landsat 5, 7, 8 and 9 merged), the formulas and the two levels of temporal aggregation of the app, and adds: the pixel filters of the app (remove the pixels with clouds, water, snow or aerosols), which are switched off there, on by default, with the QA bands of the catalog (SCL for Sentinel-2, except its clouds: Cloud Score+ >= 0.6 by default, which gave an NDVI p5 closer to the Guajares reference, r = 0.981 against 0.953, or SCL with cloud_score: null; QA_PIXEL and SR_QA_AEROSOL for Landsat; DetailedQA, FparExtra_QC, AOD_QA, ... for MODIS), and a warning when a filter removes what the variable measures (e.g. snow for NDSI), the scale and offset applied before the formulas (the app evaluates EVI, albedo and the Sentinel-2 chlorophyll on the raw bands), band names matched without case (LST_day_1km of the catalog), the flag values of MODIS masked (e.g. 250 = cloud in NDSI_Snow_Cover), Landsat 7 only until 2003-05-31, any percentile and the scale of LAI corrected to 0.1 (FIXES in catalog.py). The images are downloaded in tiles with ee.data.computePixels, split when Earth Engine finds them too big or too slow. environment.yml and requirements.txt add earthengine-api and pyyaml.

- New segm_optim/similarity.py (after run_hft.py), which replaces pas/subpas_loop_segm_optim.py and ehab_optim.py (+ _median) of the global 1 km run: compares every HFT of a cut of a scenario with every pixel of its ecoregion, with the same variables at the ecoregion scale (inVars/<set>/ecoregion, paired by name), and writes in <cut>/similarity_<pixel size>m the similarity map of every HFT (chi-square probability of the Mahalanobis distance, 0 at or below 0.001, NoData outside the ecoregion), the map of its similar areas, hri_results.csv with the landscape metrics of eHabitat+ (AveHFTSim/MedianHFTSim, NrSimPixEco, HSR, patches, aggregation...) and new columns (hft, areas, pixel size, variables, Status), then runs thdi.R and similarity_plots.R. - The similarity can be computed with a coarser pixel (ECOSIM_RESOLUTION, a multiple of the pixel of the ecoregion variables; the ecoregion variables are aggregated by their areal mean, without downloading them again). The mean and covariance of every HFT always come from the pixels of the similarity grid it covers (at least ECOSIM_MIN_COVER of their area), not from the 10 m pixels of the HFT map, so that the HFT and the ecoregion are measured with the same pixel and product.
- Fixed with respect to ehab_optim.py: pixels outside the ecoregion are NoData instead of zero vectors with a similarity; no NaN interpolation along the rows; no random noise on the HFT values, which was there because the Mahalanobis distance fails when a variable is constant (now a variable constant in the HFT or in the whole area is left out of the distance and the run says which one, and a non positive definite correlation matrix gets a small ridge); HFT and ecoregion pixels on the same grid (they could be one pixel off); threshold not rounded; HFTs with too few pixels are reported in Status; the per-variable values of an HFT are no longer inherited from the previous one.
- thdi.R rewritten with arguments (--hri, --hfts, --ecoregions, --marine, --out) for the results of similarity.py: same indices (TerrEco, MarEco, TotEco, NrHFT, SIH, THD, THR, THDI), ecoregions in any CRS, NA values allowed, SIH from the HFT areas.
- New page Similarity in the desktop app: HFTs of a scenario and cut, pixel size, options, the pixels of every HFT at that pixel size, the run with its log, and the results (THDI, metrics, map of the similarity and similar areas of every HFT, plots).
- Comparison areas: similarity.py compares the HFTs with any folder of variables with the same names (ECOSIM_AREA, --area), inside or outside the study area, in similarity_<pixel size>m_<area>; when the area has a subfolder study_area (the study area at the pixel of the area, same recipe and grid lines; get_vars_gee.py downloads it when the area does not contain the study area), the mean and covariance of every HFT come from it. similarity.comparison_areas() lists the candidate folders for the app.
- similarity.py: global ecoregion files (e.g. all of RESOLVE) are read only near the grid (they could not be reprojected far from it); ecoregion IDs without an ID field are the feature IDs + 1 in the maps and the records alike; with --id, the other IDs of a folder of results are computed again too, so that it keeps all of them.
- Every plot of eHabitat+ comes with a .csv of the same name with the data it draws (moranvar_plots.R and similarity_plots.R), separated by spaces like the other tables; r2_<id>_hfts.csv and hft_tree_<id>_<k>.csv get the columns of what is drawn.
- Desktop app: basemap Sentinel-2 cloudless 2023 (EOX, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) on every map.
- New tables of the HFTs to compare study areas (moranvar_plots.R), in the folder of the scenario with the HFTs of every cut: hft_means_<id>_<k>.csv, one row per HFT with its segments, pixels, area and the mean of its pixels in each variable (the matrix of HFTs x variables), and hft_stats_<id>_<k>.csv, one row per HFT and variable with n, mean, sd, min, quartiles and max of its pixels. The values are in the original units and the variables are named after their files without the folder of INVARS_FOLDER, so the tables of study areas made with the same variables can be joined by rows for an NMDS or a hierarchical tree of all their HFTs (example in the wiki, Interpreting the outputs). The desktop app shows hft_means in Results.
- hft_means_<id>_<k>.csv and hft_stats_<id>_<k>.csv have the column resolution (pixel size of the variables, m): HFTs made with other pixel sizes are not directly comparable, and the example of the wiki warns when the tables joined have different ones.
